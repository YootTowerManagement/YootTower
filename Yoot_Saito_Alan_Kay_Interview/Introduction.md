# Introduction

In 1993, game designer [Yutaka "Yoot" Saito](People.md#yutaka-yoot-saito-waseda-university-macworld-japan-maxis-vivarium-openbook) interviewed [Alan Kay](People.md#alan-kay-university-of-colorado-at-boulder-university-of-utah-xerox-parc-atari-apple-viewpoints-research-institute) for [MACWORLD Japan](Institutions.md#macworld-japan), for the special issue marking the Macintosh's tenth anniversary (January 1994). The conversation was recorded on cassette and later transcribed. It had only been published in Japan.

Kay covers his own path through computing history: the [Flex Machine](Technologies.md#flex-machine), [Xerox PARC](Institutions.md#xerox-parc-palo-alto-research-center), the graphical user interface, the [Dynabook](Technologies.md#dynabook) concept, and the creation of [Smalltalk](Technologies.md#smalltalk) and object-oriented programming. He and Saito also discuss networking, education, simulation, and where personal computing might go next. Saito connects these ideas to game design.

During the interview both men appear to be working from Kay's HOPL paper, [The Early History of Smalltalk](EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk.md), and its appendices as a shared reference. When Kay says "in that," "in there," or "in the appendix," the links in the transcript point to the relevant section where it can be identified.

The interview also documents Saito's role as a bridge between Japanese game and media culture and the US computing research that produced the personal computer.
